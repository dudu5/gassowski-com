Story

How I got here

For years, people brought me their stuck places. Founders who couldn't say what they actually wanted. Couples who were talking past each other. Teams that ran smart meetings and still couldn't move. I sat with them, drew pictures of the system, found the gap between what they said they needed and what was actually running them. It worked.

I was good at seeing other people's systems. I was not good at seeing my own.

The thing about mapping is that it requires distance. You need enough space from the material to see its shape. I had almost no distance from myself. I had strategies everywhere — productivity systems, goals, frameworks — and almost no honest picture of what I actually needed right now. I was swimming with my fists.

The turn came slowly, then all at once. I started treating myself the way I treated clients. I drew the map. I found the gap. I stopped optimising for the future I thought I should want and started noticing what was actually true in the present moment.

A map, in my practice, is a shared picture of what is real. Not what should be, not what we wish were true — what is. It doesn't fix anything by itself. But it gives you something to stand on. And standing on something real is how movement starts.

I am the worked example. The hardest system to see is your own.
